About Smurdy

Smurdy is a free geography quiz site that I build and run independently. I started it because I wanted map practice that was quick to open, had more than one kind of question, and let me keep making the difficulty weirder when the normal quizzes got easy.

I go by Smurdy online. I like geography and programming, and I spend a ridiculous amount of time staring at maps, planning trips, and thinking of geography challenges. A lot of features here started because I was playing the site myself and thought, "this would be better if it did this instead."

One rule I keep coming back to: if a feature makes the normal quiz slower to start, it needs a good reason.

What is on the site

  • Bordered map quizzes where you click or type countries and US states.
  • No-borders quizzes that make you remember the map without political lines.
  • Point quizzes that test which place actually contains a location.
  • Flag quizzes for the world, regions, specialty groups, and US states.
  • Country capitals and all 50 US state capitals.
  • Weak Spots, which saves misses by quiz type so the same kind of question can come back later.

Why there are so many modes

I do not think knowing a map is one skill. Recognizing France with every border visible is different from typing its name from a shape, placing it on a blank map, recognizing its flag, or remembering Paris. Smurdy keeps those as separate quizzes instead of treating one score as proof that you know everything.

Weak Spots

Weak Spots remembers both the place you missed and the kind of quiz you missed it in. A missed flag comes back as a flag. A missed no-borders question comes back without borders. If you get it right in Retry Missed, it leaves the list.

How places are counted

Country map quizzes generally follow Natural Earth's separately mapped features and group ordinary dependencies with their sovereign state. UN member and observer states are kept as separate answers, along with a small number of de facto entities that the source data already represents separately. Inclusion, naming, and displayed borders do not represent Smurdy's position on sovereignty or international recognition.

What I want to add next

The order changes constantly, but the current list includes Locate modes for flags and capitals, country-shape quizzes, major-city quizzes, Study Mode, and a Smurdy Daily challenge. I also want to keep expanding subdivisions and make the site much better for teachers without making the normal quizzes harder to start.

Feedback actually changes the site

Some of the smaller changes come from things I notice while playing, and others come directly from people who send bug reports or suggestions. If something feels wrong, confusing, or missing, send it here.
