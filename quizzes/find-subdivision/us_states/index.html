US States Without Borders Quiz: Find All 50 States

Find the Subdivisions · US States · 50 states

Find all 50 US states after the internal state lines disappear. This no-borders states quiz tests whether you know each location from coastlines, regional order, and a mental map of the United States.

What the 50-state no-borders quiz covers

The quiz includes all 50 states across the contiguous United States, Alaska, and Hawaii. State outlines are hidden during play, so the challenge focuses on absolute and relative location rather than matching familiar shapes.

Why Washington, DC is not included

Washington, DC is a federal district, not one of the 50 states, and it is not represented as a quiz area on this map. The included-place total therefore stays at exactly 50: the 48 contiguous states plus Alaska and Hawaii.

How the no-borders subdivision quiz works

A state name appears while the internal borders of the United States are hidden. Click where the state belongs using coastlines, the national outline, regional position, and nearby geographic anchors, then continue through all 50 states.

Gameplay tip: First identify the region, then estimate the state's position within that region instead of aiming from the whole national map.

Skills this no-borders subdivision quiz develops

This mode tests whether you understand the internal layout of a country rather than merely recognizing visible state or province outlines.

A reliable no-borders subdivision process

Divide the parent country into broad regions, place familiar anchors, and estimate the target's position relative to them.

Who should use this subdivision mode

Choose this after bordered subdivision maps have become comfortable.

Common no-borders subdivision mistake

Do not treat the parent-country outline as empty space; coastlines and outer borders still provide a coordinate system.

Hardest US regions without state lines

The Northeast packs many small states into a narrow area, while the Midwest and Great Plains lose the rectangular borders that normally make them recognizable. Inland states require especially strong north-south and east-west ordering.

How to study the states without borders

Build four regional chains: the Pacific and Mountain West, the Great Plains, the Mississippi and Great Lakes states, and the Atlantic coast. Practice each chain in geographic order before mixing all 50 states together.
