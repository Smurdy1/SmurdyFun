The 50-state map

This practice set covers the 50 states, the first-level political subdivisions of the United States. It focuses on internal locations, state names, regional patterns, and the shapes created by state borders.

California and Texas are easy anchors; the Northeast and the cluster around Missouri demand much closer reading.

The rectangle trap

Several western and central states look simple enough that it is tempting to remember them as interchangeable boxes. Their neighbors and relative position are usually the better clue.

How the subdivision click quiz works

The quiz names one state at a time. Click the matching subdivision on the map until you have completed all 50 states.

Start here when you're learning the internal map of the United States or getting ready for one of the harder subdivision modes.

Tip: Use neighboring subdivisions and recognizable boundary shapes as anchors.

The middle is harder than the coasts

The central and northeastern states are usually harder than the large western states because many are similar in size or packed closely together.
