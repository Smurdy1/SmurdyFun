Test your knowledge of Latin America by finding each country on a map with borders hidden.

What this Latin America quiz covers

Latin America connects Mexico, Central America, much of the Caribbean, and South America in one broad regional set. The grouping mainly follows shared Spanish, Portuguese, and French colonial language history.

How the no-borders map quiz works

The quiz names a country, but the usual political borders are removed. Click where you think it belongs by using coastlines, neighboring landforms, distance, and your memory of the regional layout. This set covers all 21 countries.

Gameplay tip: Use coastlines and the positions of nearby large countries as anchors before judging smaller inland areas.

What you'll practice

With the border lines gone, you have to remember the layout itself. Coastlines, distances, and the order of nearby places become much more important.

A good way to start

Start from something you know: a coastline, peninsula, island, or large country. Place the target relative to it instead of guessing from empty space.

When this mode helps

Move to this mode when the normal Latin America click quiz starts to feel easy and you want to see whether you actually know where everything goes.

One mistake to watch for

The map may look blank, but it still has clues. Use coastlines, peninsulas, large rivers, and countries you already know.

What makes this group challenging

The region stretches across two continents and includes both mainland countries and islands, so players must shift between several different map scales.

Study tip

Learn Central America and South America separately, then add the Caribbean countries included in the group.
